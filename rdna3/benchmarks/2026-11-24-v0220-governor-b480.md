# CPU frequency governor on the adopted profile: neutral

b480 paired the [adopted profile](2026-11-24-v0220-grdna-adoption.md) with the host CPU governor left at `powersave`
against the same profile with `performance` written to every CPU before each candidate load and restored right after
it, on dual RX 7900 XTX. Same binary, environment, expert-profile seed and cache budgets in both arms. The host has
ten physical cores and 20 logical CPUs under `intel_pstate` in active mode with the energy preference already at
`performance`, so the governor only changes the minimum frequency (800 MHz against 4 GHz). The question was whether
the short CPU bursts of each verifier window start at a reduced clock.

## Quality

The two arms are token-identical at all 26 frozen probes with identical forced rollbacks, keep the adopted profile's
reference shape, and pass the objective and scored-text checks.

## Paired result

Three alternating fresh-load pairs; pair 2 reversed the workload order. All 36 scored requests finished at 1,024
output tokens with caches off.

| Workload | powersave tok/s | performance tok/s | Throughput | TTFT | Faster pairs |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Chinese single | 124.80 | 127.00 | +1.76% | +0.44% | 2/3 |
| Chinese dual | 175.40 | 174.09 | -0.75% | -0.23% | 1/3 |
| Code single | 164.80 | 167.20 | +1.46% | +0.91% | 3/3 |
| Code dual | 239.88 | 236.54 | -1.39% | +0.82% | 1/3 |

Not adopted: the single lanes gained 1.5% to 1.8%, below the 2% bar, and the dual lanes lost 0.8% to 1.4%. The
frequency ramp is not where the remaining single-lane time goes. The governor was verified back at `powersave` for
all 20 CPUs after the window; production restoration was verified and nothing was deployed. Per-pair values:
[JSON](2026-11-24-v0220-governor-b480.json).
